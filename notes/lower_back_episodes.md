# Lower back episodes — log

**Prepared:** 3 September 2026 (updated 5 September 2026)

Recurring acute lower back pain on the bike, roughly once or twice a month, usually triggered by a sudden out-of-the-saddle effort. Typically resolves within 1–2 days. This file is the running record; add an entry for each episode so there's something concrete to show a physio or GP.

## Red flags (seek prompt medical attention if any appear)

- Pain, numbness or tingling running down a leg
- Weakness in a leg or foot
- Any change in bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the saddle area
- Pain worsening over 1–2 days rather than improving

## Action items

- [ ] Book a physio assessment (NHS MSK self-referral or private sports physio) — the frequency is the reason, not any single episode
- [ ] Ask about bike fit: saddle height, reach, and how much lumbar flexion the position demands
- [ ] Restart the strength routine from the training plan (RDL, split squat, plank, row) once fully settled — it stopped mid-June
- [ ] Until assessed: stay seated when pushing away from lights; use gears rather than standing
