### Rear wheel — pothole dent, 12 Sep 2026

- Dent in the rim wall from an unseen pothole on the Ripley Lanes Loop. Surface smooth, bead hook intact.
- Tyre seats normally, no pressure loss. No lateral wobble; slight radial hop noticeable only on smooth tarmac.
- Squeak once per revolution, only with weight on the rear (sitting upright or shifting weight back). Load-dependent and periodic, so it's the dent — rim flexing at the flat spot and a spoke crossing/nipple creaking, or the rim deflecting into a pad — not a bearing (which would be continuous and speed-related regardless of load).
- **Rideable.** The hop and the squeak are the dent and won't true out. Re-check for hairline cracks after the next few rides.
- Fix is a wheel swap, not a repair: a shop rebuild on a Paradigm SL rim costs about what an equivalent whole wheel does. See Boondocks 3 below.

### Tyres

Stock R1 Hard-Case Lite are wire-bead, 60 tpi: heavy, stiff, slow. A folding 32 mm tyre (aramid bead instead of steel wire, and with it a suppler higher-tpi casing and better compound) is the biggest free speed gain available on this bike — larger than the wheel swap — and worth doing before EPIC49 regardless of the wheel situation. Any of the candidates works with butyl tubes, TPU tubes or tubeless, and on hooked or hookless rims.

~15 W per pair ≈ 1 km/h at the same effort, ≈ 5–6 min over an 80 km Z2 ride, ≈ 8–10 min over EPIC49. Non-RS P Zero Race TLR 32 also tests at 15.8 W — avoid; the RS is the one to buy. Vittoria Corsa N.EXT has no 32 mm test; dropped from the shortlist.

Review commentary: BRR calls the P Zero RS a serious contender to replace the GP5000 S TR as the gold-standard road tyre — near-identical rolling resistance with ~10% better puncture resistance, thicker tread and 10–15% more wet grip. BikeRadar's reviewers favour the Conti for on-road grip and its balance of grip, durability and weight; one long-time Pro One user rated it for easy tubeless setup and durability.

**Recommendation:** P Zero Race TLR RS 32 if the price is within ~£10 of the Conti; otherwise GP5000 S TR 32 (typically £50–55 vs £65–75 in the UK). Skip the Pro One and the non-RS Pirelli at this width.

**Tubes vs tubeless (12 Sep discussion):** butyl (current) is simplest and slowest (~4–6 W/pair penalty vs tubeless, pinch-flat prone); TPU tubes (Tubolito / Pirelli SmarTube / Schwalbe Aerothan, ~35 g, £20–30) get most of the tubeless gain with no maintenance — preferred; tubeless is worth it only if punctures become a recurring problem, and only on the Boondocks (arrive taped and valved), not the dented Bontrager rim. Don't change setup in the last two weeks before the event.

Pressure at ~75 kg on 32s: around 60–65 psi front / 65–70 rear with tubes; ~10 psi less tubeless.

## Second wheelset: Alexrims Boondocks 3 (offered used, £50, Sep 2026)

Spec (Alexrims / retailer listings): alloy, disc only, tubeless ready, sleeve-joint rim, 30 mm deep, 21 mm internal / 24.6 mm external, 24h front and rear, 2-cross, double-butted stainless spokes. Hubs: sealed bearings (2 front / 4 rear), Shimano/SRAM 8–11-speed freehub, end caps for QR or 12/15×100 front and QR or 12×142 rear. 6-bolt or centrelock. Claimed ~1,690–1,715 g/pair. New price ~£250–300 (US$330).

Fit on the Domane: yes — 12×100 / 12×142 thru-axle caps, 6-bolt rotors and 11-speed freehub all match; the Tiagra cassette moves across with its spacer. Same 21 mm internal width as the stock rims, so the same tyres and pressures apply.

Notes:
- ~200–300 g lighter than the stock Paradigm SL / Formula wheels; a mild upgrade, not a transformation. Main value is a sound spare rear wheel and an easy tubeless setup.
- One review describes the rim wall as hookless. If so: tubeless-ready tyres only, and keep pressure ≤ ~70 psi. Confirm by looking at the bead seat (no inward lip at the top of the rim wall = hookless).
- Before paying: spin both wheels for bearing roughness and play, check true and for cracks at the spoke holes and sleeve joint, check the freehub engages cleanly and the 12 mm end caps are included, and check rotor type matches (6-bolt for this bike; centrelock needs adapters).

**Recommendation (12 Sep): buy, subject to the checks above.** Resolves the dent, the hop and the squeak in one go.

### Swap procedure (home job, no shop needed)

1. Cassette across from the stock rear wheel: lockring tool + chain whip. Keep the 1.85 mm spacer behind the cassette.
2. Rotors: if the Boondocks come bare, move the RT66s over — T25 Torx, 6 bolts each, ~6 Nm, star pattern. If they come with 6-bolt rotors fitted, leave them.
3. Fit wheels, spin, look through each caliper. If the rotor rubs: loosen the two caliper bolts, squeeze the lever, retighten. Normal with a different hub.
4. After the first ride: check thru-axles are snug and the rear cassette lockring hasn't loosened.
